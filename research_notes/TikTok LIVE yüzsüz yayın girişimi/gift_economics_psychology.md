## 1. Coin pricing (USD, TL, regions, web vs app)

### Takeaway
TikTok does not publish a coin price list. Third-party sites agree on roughly **$1.04–1.05 per 100 coins on the web (tiktok.com/coin)** and roughly **$1.33–1.53 per 100 coins in the iOS/Android app**, so the web is about 25–35% cheaper. Turkish lira prices found online run from **about 0.21 to 0.50 TL per coin**. They come from 2025 SEO pages that disagree with each other and are probably out of date after TL depreciation.

  - These are third-party figures; re-check at the checkout screen before modeling.
- The TL figures imply two things:
  - 17,500 coins = 6,200 TL works out to **≈0.354 TL/coin**.
  - In the same anecdote, $185 = 4,993 TL implies about 27 TL/USD. That exchange rate places the anecdote around 2023–2024, so it is dated.
- Per-coin TL prices from 2025 sources (0.21–0.50 TL) cannot be turned into a current USD figure without a verified Nov 2026 TRY/USD rate, which I did not collect.
- For a Turkish audience, the coin price in local currency is the relevant cost of a gift. If the gift catalogue (coin cost) is global but coin prices are regionally adjusted, a "1,000-coin Galaxy" can cost a Turkish viewer a different USD amount than a US viewer, while the creator's Diamond value is the same. That last point is an inference that I could not verify officially.

### Gaps

- No verified data on whether the web recharge is offered in Turkey at a discount versus app stores.
- No current TRY/USD rate was collected, so no USD-normalized Turkish price can be given.

## 3. Diamonds, creator share, cash value, withdrawal, agencies

### Takeaway
TikTok says only that gifts convert to Diamonds "at a rate of conversion" it sets "in its absolute and sole discretion". It has never published the split. The working industry figure is **about $0.005 per coin gifted** reaching the creator, i.e. ~50% of the coin's nominal $0.01 value. Digiday reported a **50% cut** in April 2025. A BBC test in 2022 found **69%** of what the gifter paid was retained. Measured against what the **viewer actually paid**, the creator's share is roughly **33–48%**, depending on web or app purchase. Receiving gifts and withdrawing requires being 18+. Withdrawal minimums are not reliably documented.

### Cited Findings

- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY journalism, 2022] BBC investigation (Oct 2022) of Syrian refugee families begging on LIVE:
  - BBC ran a test account and found TikTok took **69%** of the value of the gifts.
  - TikTok said its commission was "significantly less than 70%" but did not specify an amount.
  - Middlemen supplying phones took about 35% of what remained.
  - — [ACS Information Age](https://ia.acs.org.au/article/2022/tiktok-takes-70--of-livestream-donations.html); [Fortune](https://www.fortune.com/2022/10/12/tiktok-china-exploitation-bytedance-children-livestreaming-refugees-charity-donations-syria-bbc); [PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2022/10/13/tiktok-is-profiting-from-live-streams-of-syrian-refugees-begging/)

### Inferences (computed)
Assumption A1 (unofficial): the creator receives **c ≈ $0.005 per coin gifted**. This is equivalent whether framed as "1 coin → 1 Diamond worth $0.005" or "50% of coins → Diamonds worth $0.01".

- **Creator share of viewer spend** = c / p:
  - Web: 0.005 / 0.0105 ≈ **47.6%**
  - App at the low price: 0.005 / 0.0133 ≈ **37.6%**
  - App at the high price: 0.005 / 0.0153 ≈ **32.7%**
  - The BBC 2022 test (31% to creator) is close to the app-purchase end of this range.
- **Per 1,000 coins gifted**: the creator gets ≈ 1,000 × $0.005 = **$5.00** gross. Before that, viewers spent ≈ $10.50 (web) to $13.30–15.30 (app).
- **Per gift, creator gross at A1**:

  | Gift | Coins | Creator gross |
  |---|---|---|
  | Rose | 1 | $0.005 |
  | Finger Heart | 5 | $0.025 |
  | Doughnut | 30 | $0.15 |
  | Hand Hearts | 100 | $0.50 |
  | Corgi | 299 | $1.50 |
  | Galaxy | 1,000 | $5.00 |
  | Lion | 29,999 | ~$150 |
  | Universe | 44,999 | ~$225 |

- **Coins needed for a target income**: coins = target $ / 0.005. Example: $1,000/month needs 200,000 coins/month, or ≈ 6,667 coins/day over 30 days. That is about 6.7 Galaxies a day or about 1,333 Finger Hearts a day. This is arithmetic only, not a forecast.
- **Net to creator** = gross × (1 − agency commission, if any in the contract) − payout-method fees − income tax. None of these were verified for Turkey.
- Compared with Twitch Bits (streamer gets ~71% of the historical $1.40 viewer price), TikTok's creator share of viewer spend is materially lower.

### Gaps
- There is no official TikTok statement of the Diamond-to-USD rate or the creator split. The Virtual Items policy page could not be fetched.
- No verified withdrawal minimum, payout methods or fees for Turkey (PayPal and bank availability in TR not confirmed).
- I could not determine whether the Diamond value differs by region. The Universe 44,999 vs 34,999 conflict also affects the high-tier figures.

## 4. Real-world revenue evidence (platform-level and creator-level)

### Takeaway
The most solid scale numbers come from **TikTok itself in April 2025**, reported by Digiday and Music Ally:
- **$10M/day** in LIVE creator revenue and **$63M in the prior 30 days**
- **80.4%** of that came from creators with fewer than 50k followers
- **400k+ creators** go live daily, with **30M+ daily viewers**

These are company-supplied and unaudited. Investigations (BBC 2022, the Utah lawsuit in 2025) show large platform cuts and abuse risks. I found no reliable median-creator earnings figure.

### Cited Findings
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY journalism quoting TikTok, Apr 2025] At an April 24, 2025 creator mixer at its New York HQ, TikTok said creators collectively generate **$10 million a day** from livestreaming. It also gave these figures:
  - LIVE creators generated **$63M in the last 30 days**
  - **80.4%** of revenue came from creators with **<50,000 followers**
  - **400,000+** creators go live daily

### Inferences (computed)
- **Average per active creator (rough)**: $10M/day ÷ 400,000 creators/day ≈ **$25 per creator per day**.
  - This is a mean, so a few top earners pull it up heavily (see whales, section 6).
  - It is unclear whether $10M is gross gift value or creator payout, and whether it is global or US-only. The figure was given at a US event.
  - $63M/30 days ≈ $2.1M/day, which is inconsistent with "$10M/day" unless the 30-day figure covers a subset (e.g. US only) or a different measure. **Flag this inconsistency.**
- The "80.4% from <50k-follower creators" claim suggests gifting is not limited to big accounts. This is favorable for a new faceless stream, but it is the company's own framing.
- BIGO ARPPU of ≈ $222.6/quarter ≈ **$74/month per paying user** is spend per payer on a gift-centric social-live app.
  - At the TikTok web coin price, that equals about $74 / $0.0105 ≈ **7,000 coins/month per payer**.
  - This is a cross-platform analogy, not TikTok data.
  - BIGO's falling revenue and ARPPU over 2023–2025 show gifting markets can shrink.

### Gaps
- No official or reliable **median** TikTok LIVE creator income. No distribution of creator earnings.
- No TikTok-specific paying-viewer conversion rate or ARPPU.
- Could not access the BBC original or Digiday full text to confirm the exact wording ("revenue" vs "gifts", geography).

## 5. Gifting motivations and behavioral design (with ethical limits)

### Takeaway
Research on Douyin, Inke, Twitch and other Chinese platforms consistently finds gifting is driven by **social recognition and status**, **reciprocity and acknowledgment from the streamer**, **intimacy or parasocial bonds**, and **visible social proof**. Two caveats matter for design:
- Status gifting is **positional**: fake or seeded tips can *crowd out* real tipping among heavy tippers.
- **Perceived audience size reduces** willingness to buy expensive gifts.

TikTok forbids gambling-like mechanics and manipulative gift solicitation, and requires 18+ for gifting.

### Cited Findings
- [ACADEMIC, qualitative] Klug & Qiu (2021, AoIR SPIR), Douyin:
  - Viewers use gifts mainly as social appreciation and support in parasocial relationships.
  - They tend toward smaller, frequent gifts to maintain presence.
  - Seeing others gift, and streamer appreciation of gifts, encourage continued gifting.
  - — [AoIR SPIR](https://spir.aoir.org/ojs/index.php/spir/article/view/12194); [arXiv 2108.05960](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.05960)
- [ACADEMIC] Lee, Yen, Wang & Fu (2019), on tip-based live streams:
  - Viewers are motivated by streamers' reciprocal acts: streamers engage in social interaction with tippers.
  - Viewers tip large amounts "to draw attention from the crowd or to promote content they prefer".
  - Gift design shapes interaction.
  - — [Illinois Experts](https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/understanding-how-digital-gifing-influences-social-interaction-on/); [PDF](https://dwnnis.github.io/static/lee2019.pdf)
- [ACADEMIC, field experiment] Dai, Lu & Chen (2025): on a Chinese live-streaming platform, synthetic viewers sending random tips **reduced (crowded out)** peers' tipping and chatting. The effect was strongest among heavy tippers, consistent with status motives. Likes, which show no identity, were not affected. — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/10591478231224948?mi=ehikzz); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/380482962_Crowding-Out_in_Content_Monetization_Under_Pay_What_You_Want_Evidence_From_Live_Streaming)
- [ACADEMIC, natural experiment] Chaudhry, Wang & Ouyang (2025), Twitch community gifting:
  - Within 5 minutes of receiving a gift, recipients showed a 69% chance of an additional message to peers and a 35% chance of an additional message to the streamer.
  - They also showed a 5% chance of gifting a subscription onward.
  - They were *not* more likely to tip the streamer.
  - — [SAGE / J. Interactive Marketing](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/10949968241257891); [AMA research insight](https://ama.org/research-insights/research-insight-how-community-gifting-on-twitch-affects-user-engagement)
- [ACADEMIC] Cao et al. (2022, Frontiers in Psychology), on moving from cheap to expensive gifts:
  - **Intimacy with the streamer** increases the shift.
  - **Perceived network (audience) size** and **perceived financial risk** decrease it.
  - The paper notes most users buy only cheap gifts.
  - — [PMC9748564](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9748564/)
- [ACADEMIC] Research on Chinese platforms notes a top-gifter leaderboard visible to all viewers encourages repeated giving. — [Lu et al. 2018, arXiv 1803.06032](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.06032)
- [SECONDARY, platform data] TikTok Fan Club: viewers join by sending a gift and progress by watching, commenting and gifting. TikTok said active Fan Club ("team") members sent **3× more gifts** and watched **4× longer** than the average follower. — [Social Media Today (2024)](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/tiktok-adds-live-fan-clubs-to-drive-livestream-engagement/751564/)
- [THIRD-PARTY] LIVE Match ("PK battle"):
  - A timed head-to-head scored by gift value.
  - Sources conflict on multipliers. A developer library lists x2/x3 booster cards; another site says there is no official double-points multiplier.
  - — [tikdata](https://tikdata.live/guides/tiktok-live-battles-explained/); [npm @tiktool/live](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@tiktool/live); [ttcalculator](https://ttcalculator.net/learn/tiktok-live-battles/)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY, partial] TikTok's LIVE Monetization Guidelines state that "content, behavior, or activities that seek to manipulate viewer engagement or monetization features are ineligible for monetization". — [TikTok LIVE Monetization Guidelines](https://www.tiktok.com/live/creators/en-US/rules_and_guidance/live_monetization_guidelines)
- [THIRD-PARTY reading of TikTok rules]
  - **Gift-baiting** is prohibited: tricking viewers, asking mechanically and repeatedly, emotional pressure, or soliciting for illness or financial need.
  - **Gambling** is prohibited: raffles, lotteries, or spin-the-wheel/random draws where a gift buys a chance at money or money-like prizes.
  - Penalties escalate from warnings to monetization restrictions, including LIVE Gifts, LIVE Match and rankings.
  - — [tiklivegames](https://tiklivegames.io/articles/tiktok-live-gift-game-rules); [TikFinity blog](https://blog.tikfinity.com/tiktok-live-rules/); [streamer.guide](https://streamer.guide/guides/tiktok-live-rules)
- [SECONDARY] Concern about interactive-gift formats: "many interactive triggers are linked to virtual gifts, which viewers buy with real money." — [Canberra Times](https://www.canberratimes.com.au/story/9389858/the-audience-is-the-controller-tikfinity-and-the-new-live-format/)

### Inferences
Design levers supported by the evidence above:
1. **Recognition**: say the gifter's name, show it on screen, add a hall of fame. Supported by Lee 2019, Klug & Qiu, and the leaderboard research.
2. **Agency**: the gift visibly changes the game state. Supported by Lee 2019 ("promote content they prefer").
3. **Reciprocity / acknowledgment**: give an immediate, personal thank-you. Supported by Lee 2019 and Klug & Qiu.
4. **Make big audiences feel intimate**: use name callouts and small-group "teams" to counter the negative effect of audience size (Cao 2022).
5. **Never seed fake gifts or bots.** They crowd out real status-seeking gifters (Dai et al. 2025) and would likely break TikTok's anti-manipulation rule.
6. **Collective goals and community gifting** raise chat engagement but not necessarily tipping (Chaudhry 2025). Use them for retention, not as a main revenue lever.
7. **Avoid chance-based outcomes tied to gifts** (wheels or random rewards with value) to stay clear of the gambling prohibition and loot-box ethics concerns.

Ethically, the stream should:
- Keep gifts deterministic (gift X → effect Y), never "a chance to win".
- Avoid targeting minors. Gifting is 18+, but minors still watch.
- Avoid pressure tactics such as countdowns that imply loss for not paying, repeated requests, or financial-need appeals.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study specific to TikTok (non-Douyin) gifting motivation was found.
- No quantitative evidence on streaks/combos, FOMO timers or loss-aversion mechanics specifically in live gifting. These are inferred from general behavioral economics, not sourced here.
- I could not confirm whether the official LIVE Monetization Guidelines explicitly name "gift-baiting" or spin-the-wheel. These come from third-party summaries; the official page was not fetchable.

## 6. Interaction design for gift tiers; whales; ARPPU; conversion benchmarks

### Takeaway
Gift revenue in live streaming is **extremely concentrated**:
- **Inke**: 20% of viewers produced **97%** of gifts (Tu et al. 2018).
- **Bilibili** (non-peer-reviewed analysis): 1.3% of payers produced 73% of revenue.

Small gifts drive participation and social proof, and large gifts drive revenue. The best paying-user benchmark found is BIGO's ~1.5M paying users and ≈$223/quarter ARPPU. No TikTok-specific conversion rate exists publicly.

### Cited Findings
- [ACADEMIC] Tu et al. (2018), Inke platform: **20% of viewers contributed 97% of gifts**, and 77% of viewers only watch the top three streamers. A follow-up finding says only 2.9% of users gave more than $50. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/326051046_Who_Is_Earning_Understanding_and_Modeling_the_Virtual_Gifts_Behavior_of_Users_in_Live_Streaming_Economy); [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Who-Is-Earning-Understanding-and-Modeling-the-Gifts-Tu-Yan/7f2a8fce6effa99a444ddd0c9ee2eb70ca13c6d0)
- [ACADEMIC, citing survey] Cao et al. (2022) cite a survey in which 20% of users bought 97% of virtual gifts. — [PMC9748564](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9748564/)
- [ANECDOTAL / non-peer-reviewed analysis] Bilibili singing and chat rooms (~1,200 hours of transcribed streams):
  - Core big spenders (¥500+ cumulative) were **1.3% of paying viewers** but **73.2% of revenue**.
  - The median spend to be #1 for an evening was only ¥249.
  - — [yage.ai](https://yage.ai/live-streaming-3-en.html)
- [ACADEMIC, adjacent industry] Among adolescent gamers, the top 10% of spenders ("whales") accounted for 61.4% of all spend. — [PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/free-to-play-video-games-carry-heavy-financial-costs-for-a-vulnerable-minority-of-teens/)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] BIGO: about 1.50–1.54M paying users per quarter and ARPPU of $222.6–222.8 per quarter in H2 2025. - [SECONDARY, unverified] An Ipsos survey reportedly found 68% of surveyed TikTok users had tried gifting on LIVE. The sample and methodology are unknown. — [TTS Vibes](https://ttsvibes.com/statistics/tiktok-live-follower-conversion-rate/)
- [THIRD-PARTY] Gift-game tools fire actions on a specific gift or a min-coin threshold, with optional "repeat with gift combos" so a combo streak triggers the effect repeatedly. — [toktutorials](https://www.toktutorials.com/post/how-to-set-up-tikfinity-for-tiktok-live-in-2026-beginner-guide-for-pc-and-mac); [TikFinity](https://tikfinity.com/); [wgmojo](https://wgmojo.gg/widgets/)
- [ANECDOTAL] Third-party Minecraft/TikTok integrations tier effects by coin value: small gifts spawn minor mobs or items, larger gifts trigger bigger events. — [wgmojo Minecraft](https://wgmojo.gg/minecraft/how-tiktok-gifts-control-minecraft/)

### Inferences
Proposed tier design. This is design synthesis, not measured data.

| Tier | Coins | Creator gross at A1 | Role | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Small | 1–5 | $0.005–0.025 | Participation, social proof, combo fuel | Instant, visible, cheap effect (+1 point, spawn a small object, name flash). Combos stack ("x10 streak"). Reward volume (Klug & Qiu: frequent small gifts maintain presence). |
| Medium | 30–300 | $0.15–1.50 | Agency | Changes game state meaningfully (power-up, choose next level, vote override, named on-screen character for N minutes). |
| Large | 1,000+ | $5+ | Status and spectacle | Rare, long-lasting, personalized event (gifter's name on a permanent "hall of fame", boss fight, scene change, custom shout-out). Status is positional, so keep a visible top-gifter board. |

- **Revenue model template** (fill with your own measured data; do not assume values):
  - R_creator/stream = Σ over gifters (coins_i) × c, with c ≈ $0.005 (A1).
  - Decompose as: coins = U × r_pay × C̄_payer, where:
    - U = unique viewers per stream (from LIVE Center)
    - r_pay = share of unique viewers who send ≥1 gift
    - C̄_payer = mean coins per paying viewer
  - Because of concentration (top 1–20% of payers producing about 70–97% of value), model it as: R ≈ (coins from top-k gifters) + (long-tail coins). Report the median and the top-gifter share separately. The mean is unstable with few whales.
- Practical implication: a stream can be sustained by a **handful of repeat high gifters**. Retention and recognition of repeat gifters (Fan Club levels, hall of fame, remembering names) matter more than raw viewer counts.

- BIGO's paying-ratio denominator (live-streaming MAU) was not captured, so no conversion rate can be computed from it.
- No data was found on what specific small/medium/large effects top interactive TikTok streams use, or on their measured outcomes.

## 9. Turkey vs international audience (pricing power, geo-targeting, time zones)

### Takeaway
Coin prices in Turkey are regionally set in TL and vary across sources. TikTok gives no official documentation of how LIVE audiences are geographically assigned. Vendor claims conflict: some say IP decides the regional pool, others say several signals do (account region, SIM/carrier, language, app-store country). Using proxies or VPNs carries **account-restriction risk** (anecdotal reports of LIVE being disabled). Reaching US evenings from Turkey means streaming in the **Turkish small hours**. UK/EU evenings fall in Turkish late evening.

### Cited Findings
- [THIRD-PARTY / vendor, conflicting] One VPN vendor claims TikTok assigns a LIVE's regional audience pool from the device IP when the stream starts, so a non-US IP routes the stream to viewers in that region. GeeLark says TikTok does not choose the audience country from IP alone, and that changing IP, location or time zone cannot directly choose your audience country. — [vpntous](https://vpntous.com/blog/why-your-tiktok-live-stream-gets-lower-us-viewership-than-your-regular-videos); [GeeLark](https://www.geelark.com/blog/tiktok-views-wrong-country/); [monetizednow](https://monetizednow.com/tiktok-region-vpn-views)
- [THIRD-PARTY] The account region is set at signup and changes only after consistent signals from a new country over about three months (vendor claim). — [vpntous region lock](https://vpntous.com/guides/tiktok-90-day-region-lock)
- [ANECDOTAL] A forum user reports a LIVE was shown mainly to their own country. Switching between a proxy and local IP led TikTok to disable features including LIVE access. — [BlackHatWorld](https://www.blackhatworld.com/seo/tiktok-would-only-show-my-live-stream-to-my-country-help.1714450/)

### Inferences
- **Time-zone arithmetic**:
  - Turkey is UTC+3 all year with no DST. This is general knowledge, not sourced above.
  - A "7–11 pm local" prime-time window in Turkish time:

    | Target region | Winter (now, Nov 2026) | Summer |
    |---|---|---|
    | US East (EST UTC−5 / EDT UTC−4) | 03:00–07:00 TR | 02:00–06:00 TR |
    | US West (PST UTC−8 / PDT UTC−7) | 06:00–10:00 TR | 05:00–09:00 TR |
    | UK (GMT / BST) | 22:00–02:00 TR | 21:00–01:00 TR |
    | Central Europe (CET / CEST) | 21:00–01:00 TR | 20:00–00:00 TR |

  - So **UK/EU evenings are workable from Turkey**. US prime time requires overnight streaming, or targeting US afternoons, which fall in Turkish evenings (e.g. 1–5 pm ET = 21:00–01:00 TR in winter).
  - A faceless, automated or semi-automated game stream fits overnight slots better than a host-led show.
- **Geo-reach strategy**: since audience assignment is unclear, the low-risk approach is to stream from a legitimately registered account in Turkey and measure viewer countries in LIVE analytics. Using VPNs or proxies to fake location risks enforcement and is not recommended.
- **Monetization from a Turkish audience**: the creator's Diamond value per coin is (probably) the same regardless of the gifter's country. The barrier for Turkish viewers is purchasing power: the TL cost of coins relative to local income. A Turkish audience may therefore produce more small gifts and fewer large ones. This is an inference, not measured.
